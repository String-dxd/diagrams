Assign each plant’s dispersal method

Choose the method represented by each plant. Switch on repeated methods when two or more plants should use the same method. With repeats off, choosing a method already assigned to another plant swaps the two assignments.

Student version: you choose each plant’s dispersal method. The map scale, labels, legend, plant symbols and water-body shape remain fixed. You may drag the parent plants and seedlings. A parent released in an unsuitable position moves to the nearest plausible location; validation also corrects invalid parent and seedling placements.

Plant Shape Method Seedlings

Drag parent plants and individual seedlings on the map. Parent markers are filled; seedlings are smaller outline shapes. Repeated dispersal methods are allowed. Each plant must use a different shape. A water-dispersed parent must sit beside the water boundary without overlapping it. River and coastline parents also need enough downstream space; lakes use a shoreline scatter with no flow direction. Use the standard waterline controls to adjust regular layouts. Generated questions use transparent white areas.

Advanced water-layout controls

For teachers only. These controls guide where the selected river, coastline, inlet or lake is generated.

Mark the actual route or area. Cells connect through shared sides. The layout engine reads the selected cells, identifies the water feature, asks for confirmation, then follows the selection: a river uses a narrow connected route between two map edges, a coastline uses a broad edge-connected water area, an inlet uses one edge-connected water area extending inland, and a lake uses an enclosed filled area that does not touch an edge.

How to mark each water feature
River

Mark a narrow continuous route. It may bend, and it must meet two map edges. The generated river follows this route.

Coastline

Shade the connected water area from a map edge inward. The boundary between selected and unselected cells becomes the coastline.

Inlet

Shade one connected water area that touches one map edge and reaches inland. It will be treated as an inlet and generated with coastline rules.

Lake

Shade one enclosed connected area away from all map edges. Its outer boundary becomes the lake shoreline.
